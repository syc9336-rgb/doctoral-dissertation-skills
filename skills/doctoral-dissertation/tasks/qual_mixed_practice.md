# Qualitative, Mixed-Method, and Practice Translation

# Qualitative research

## Purpose

Qualitative evidence should add explanatory value, not imitate quantitative validation.

It may:

- explain mechanisms;
- identify conditions;
- reveal processes;
- capture contradictions;
- surface new concepts;
- explain why quantitative outcomes diverge.

## Method facts to freeze

- participant N;
- recruitment;
- inclusion;
- interview/observation mode;
- duration;
- recording;
- consent;
- transcription;
- coding;
- theme construction;
- stopping logic;
- anonymization.

Do not allow different chapters to report different versions.

## Avoid pseudo-quantification

Do not write:

> 80% of participants believed…

for small qualitative samples unless the design explicitly warrants frequency reporting.

Prefer:

- most participants;
- several participants;
- recurring pattern;
- contrasting cases;
- minority position.

## Theme headings should express findings

Weak:

> Theme 3: Purchase intention

Stronger:

> Theme 3: Recognition did not automatically translate into purchase.

## Theme paragraph architecture

1. theme proposition;
2. meaning;
3. participant evidence;
4. recurring pattern;
5. variation/counterexample;
6. researcher interpretation;
7. relation to the RQ/other evidence.

Quotes are evidence, not analysis.

# Mixed methods

## Multiple methods are not automatically mixed methods

Integration occurs only when evidence types enter one reasoning unit.

Use a joint display when useful:

| Evidence A | Evidence B | Relationship | Integrated interpretation | Output |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Relationship types:

- convergence;
- complementarity/deepening;
- dissonance;
- expansion.

Do not force agreement.

## Do not over-explain the integration process in the thesis body

Present the integrated scholarly result.

Avoid:

> We first compared the quantitative and qualitative evidence and then derived…

Prefer:

> The two evidence streams converge on X but diverge on Y, indicating that…

# Practice/design translation

If the dissertation includes a design, intervention, framework, product, platform, educational program, or practice guideline, distinguish status precisely:

1. proposed;
2. developed;
3. reviewed;
4. tested;
5. validated;
6. implemented.

Do not call a proposal “validated.”

## Evidence-to-principle matrix

For each practice principle record:

| Principle | Empirical evidence | Qualitative evidence | Theoretical basis | Action | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

A principle should specify:

- trigger condition;
- responsible actor;
- action;
- evidence needed;
- evaluation criterion;
- risk if ignored.

“Improve user experience” is not an actionable doctoral-level principle.
