# Literature Review, Theory, and Hypotheses

## Unit of organization: the problem, not the author

Do not write author queues:

> A found… B argued… C reported…

Synthesize:

> Most studies report a positive association under condition X. Evidence becomes less consistent under Y, where differences in measurement and sample composition appear to matter.

## Every literature subsection should answer

1. What is known?
2. What is contested?
3. Why do findings differ?
4. What remains unresolved?
5. What position does the dissertation take?

## Concept definition

A mature concept subsection should include:

1. major definitions;
2. differences from adjacent concepts;
3. selected definition;
4. reason for selection;
5. how the concept is used in this dissertation.

Do not repeat the full definition in later hypothesis and discussion sections.

## Theory subsection

For each theory:

1. core mechanism;
2. what it explains;
3. why it fits the research problem;
4. what part of the dissertation uses it;
5. what it does not explain.

Delete theories that never do analytical work later.

## Research gaps

A meaningful gap can be:

- theoretical mechanism;
- conflicting evidence;
- context boundary;
- temporal/process gap;
- measurement gap;
- methodological identification gap;
- cross-level gap;
- practical/disciplinary translation gap.

“Few studies in region X” is not automatically a strong theoretical gap.

## Hypothesis reasoning

The thesis needs theoretical support, but should not narrate the author's derivation process.

Avoid:

> From the above we further derive H1.

Prefer:

> A changes the information basis on which B is judged. Consistent evidence across related settings links stronger A to higher B. Accordingly:

> H1: A is positively associated with B.

### Do not repeat the theory

If the theory was already established earlier:

- refer to its mechanism implicitly or briefly;
- add only the relationship-specific reasoning;
- state the hypothesis.

## Do not preset results

Avoid:

> To prove H1…

Use:

> To test H1…

## Literature depth

Depth comes from:

- classification;
- comparison;
- contradiction;
- mechanism;
- conditions;
- evolution;
- synthesis.

Not from citation count.
