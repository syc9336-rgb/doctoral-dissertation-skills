# Methods, Results, and Discussion

# Methods

## Methods report research facts, not author thought history

Methods may and should report the actual process:

- recruitment;
- assignment;
- measurement;
- intervention/task;
- interviews;
- cleaning;
- analysis.

Do not report:

- what was originally planned but abandoned;
- repeated failed attempts;
- manuscript revision choices;
- strategies chosen “to make results better.”

Exception: if iterative development itself is the research method and is analyzed as data.

## Every method component should answer

1. What was done?
2. Why was this method appropriate for the RQ?
3. How was it executed?
4. How was quality controlled?
5. What evidence did it produce?

## Sample reporting

Specify:

- target population;
- recruitment;
- time;
- initial N;
- exclusions;
- final N;
- assignment/randomization status;
- relevance to the RQ.

Do not say only “the sample was sufficient.”

## Analysis strategy

Organize by RQ rather than software list.

Prefer:

> RQ1 was examined using… RQ2 was assessed using…

Software and versions are implementation details.

## No scattered defensive explanation

Methods can state a factual design boundary:

> Participants were recruited through non-probability sampling.

Do not immediately add:

> Therefore the findings should be interpreted cautiously.

Put inferential consequences in the limitations section unless essential.

# Results

## Results answer; tables store detail

Body text should report:

- direction;
- magnitude;
- uncertainty;
- hypothesis/RQ status;
- important comparison.

Tables/figures contain complete numerical detail.

Do not narrate every cell.

## Do not hide non-significant or counterintuitive findings

Report them accurately.

Do not equate non-significance with “no relationship.”

## No limitation tails

Avoid:

> X was significant, but because the sample was limited the result should be interpreted cautiously.

Report the result cleanly. Discuss limitations later.

## End results with substantive unresolved questions

A strong results chapter can identify:

- findings that fit expectations;
- findings that do not;
- divergent outcomes;
- multi-method inconsistencies;
- mechanisms that coefficients cannot explain.

Do not announce “the next chapter.”  
State the unresolved scholarly issue.

# Discussion

## Discussion is not translated results

Use the sequence:

1. what the finding means;
2. plausible mechanism;
3. relation to existing literature;
4. theoretical consequence;
5. substantive boundary if necessary.

Do not repeat detailed statistics unless needed.

## Four relations to prior research

A finding may:

- support;
- extend;
- qualify;
- challenge.

Do not force every finding into “consistent with previous studies.”

## Counterintuitive findings

Do not hide them or spin them into support.

Examine:

- construct distinction;
- measurement;
- context;
- sample;
- boundary condition;
- alternative mechanism.

Then state the scholarly consequence.

## No automatic “future research is needed”

Do not end every discussion paragraph with:

> Further research is required.

Reserve future research for the dedicated section unless an unresolved issue is itself part of the immediate theoretical point.

## Do not self-censor strong findings

If evidence is robust, use direct language:

- “the findings show”;
- “the analysis identifies”;
- “this distinction extends…”;
- “the evidence supports…”

Do not stack hedge words.
