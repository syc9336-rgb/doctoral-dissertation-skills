# Doctoral Dissertation Skill

Use this skill for **doctoral dissertation production as a complete system**, not merely for prose expansion.

The skill converts a real research project into a defensible chain:

> research facts → evidence → research questions → chapter functions → section judgments → paragraph reasoning → precise prose → auditable manuscript

## Non-negotiable rules

### 1. Evidence outranks the planned outline

Do not preserve an old chapter, study stage, validation claim, or contribution merely because it appeared in a proposal.

When planned scope and completed evidence conflict:

1. use the completed evidence;
2. revise the claim;
3. change the chapter function;
4. move incomplete work to future research if necessary;
5. never invent missing evidence to preserve the old structure.

### 2. The dissertation body presents the **finished state**, not the author's formation process

The body should contain:

- established research facts;
- mature theoretical relationships;
- completed procedures;
- actual results;
- evidence-supported interpretation;
- evidence-supported contributions.

The body should normally **not** contain:

- how the author gradually arrived at an idea;
- how the manuscript was revised;
- what was originally planned and later changed;
- what the author feared reviewers might question;
- meta-writing instructions;
- explicit derivation narration such as “from the above we can further derive…”;
- repeated explanations inserted “to avoid misunderstanding.”

Research procedures that actually occurred belong in Methods.  
Author thinking/revision history does not.

### 3. Do not pre-defend the manuscript

Avoid scattering sentences such as:

- “It should be noted that…”
- “To avoid misunderstanding…”
- “The result should be interpreted cautiously…”
- “Other factors cannot be ruled out…”
- “Further research is still required…”

Put genuine limitations in the designated limitations section, unless a boundary is essential to correctly understand the immediate result.

### 4. Rigor does not mean automatic weakening

Do not turn every supported finding into:

- “may to some extent suggest”;
- “perhaps”;
- “appears to”;
- “provides some reference”;
- “offers a certain degree of inspiration.”

Match language strength to evidence strength.

When evidence is adequate, state the scholarly judgment directly.

### 5. Do not preset the desired result

Methods investigate questions; they do not “prove” a desired conclusion.

Prefer:

- examine;
- compare;
- assess;
- test;
- identify;
- estimate.

Avoid:

- prove;
- demonstrate superiority;
- ensure the hypothesis holds;
- design the analysis to obtain a preferred result.

### 6. Never carry a previous dissertation's content into a new one

Do not inherit prior:

- cases;
- samples;
- variables;
- products;
- stimuli;
- interview topics;
- hypotheses;
- software choices;
- method combinations;
- chapter titles.

Reuse only **workflow and writing rules**.

### 7. One chapter = one major problem

Use the five-level writing architecture:

- **Chapter:** solves one major problem.
- **Section:** solves one class of sub-problem.
- **Subsection:** establishes one central scholarly judgment.
- **Paragraph:** completes one reasoning move.
- **Sentence:** carries one primary information function.

### 8. Every core conclusion needs an evidence anchor

For every central claim, identify its concrete support:

- a dataset;
- a model;
- a table;
- a figure;
- a quotation/theme;
- an observation;
- a source group;
- a validated artifact or procedure.

If no anchor exists, delete, downgrade, or obtain evidence.

# Mandatory startup artifacts

Create these internal control tables before large-scale writing.

## A. Frozen Facts Table

Lock the current valid facts:

- title;
- degree/discipline;
- research object;
- RQs;
- datasets;
- sample sizes;
- recruitment;
- randomization status;
- instruments;
- qualitative materials;
- analytical methods;
- completed analyses;
- incomplete planned work;
- ethics facts;
- appendix assets;
- current master manuscript;
- required languages;
- institutional formatting requirements.

## B. Claim–Evidence Matrix

For each strong claim, record:

- claim;
- evidence required;
- evidence actually available;
- risk;
- final treatment.

## C. RQ–Evidence–Method–Chapter–Conclusion Matrix

Every RQ must have:

- data/evidence;
- method;
- result;
- chapter location;
- final answer;
- theoretical/practical output.

If a row cannot be completed, the dissertation is not closed.

# Revision priority

Use this order:

1. academic integrity / factual conflict;
2. data, citation, footnote, numbering, and other systemic defects;
3. missing evidence;
4. inference and method problems;
5. chapter logic;
6. language;
7. formatting.

Never spend major effort beautifying layout while evidence or factual claims remain unstable.

---

# Final quality principle

A mature dissertation should read as if the research has reached a coherent scholarly form.

Readers should see:

> what the problem is → what evidence exists → what the evidence shows → why it matters → what knowledge follows

They should not see:

> how the author was thinking → how the draft changed → what the author is afraid of → what the next section is going to do → repeated defensive explanations

The target is **strong but warranted scholarly writing**:
clear where evidence is strong, precise where evidence is limited, and free of unnecessary self-weakening.
