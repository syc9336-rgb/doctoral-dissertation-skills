# Forbidden or High-Risk Writing Patterns

These are not absolute lexical bans. They are default review triggers.

# 1. Meta-writing / process narration

Audit:

- This chapter will…
- The next section will…
- The previous section discussed…
- Below we discuss…
- To make the logic clearer…
- To facilitate understanding…
- On the basis of the above, we next…
- We further derive…

Why risky:
They expose writing operations rather than scholarship.

# 2. Defensive explanation

Audit:

- It should be noted that…
- It must be emphasized that…
- To avoid misunderstanding…
- To avoid over-interpretation…
- This result should be interpreted cautiously…
- Other factors cannot be ruled out…
- Further validation is needed…

Why risky:
Scattered caveats interrupt argument and weaken the manuscript.

Action:
Move genuine limitations to the limitations section unless immediate interpretation would otherwise be wrong.

# 3. Over-hedging

Audit combinations such as:

- may to some extent;
- perhaps;
- appears;
- preliminary;
- possibly;
- offers some reference;
- has a certain implication.

Why risky:
Supported findings lose theoretical force.

# 4. Preset-result language

Avoid:

- to prove;
- to demonstrate superiority;
- to ensure the hypothesis;
- to validate effectiveness when validation has not occurred.

Use:

- examine;
- test;
- compare;
- assess;
- identify.

# 5. Project-management traces

Delete from final body:

- original version;
- revised version;
- current materials;
- to be supplemented;
- not yet available;
- later addition;
- current stage;
- based on the files provided;
- to address reviewer comments.

# 6. Promotional language

Audit:

- revolutionary;
- groundbreaking;
- major breakthrough;
- fills the blank;
- first ever;
- transformative;
- extremely important.

Require concrete comparative evidence.

# 7. Empty transition paragraphs

Delete paragraphs whose only role is:

> Based on the above, the following section discusses…

# 8. Automatic limitation tails after results

Avoid:

> X was significant, but the result should be interpreted cautiously because…

Report the result. Put general limitations later.

# 9. Author-queue literature review

Avoid:

> A said… B said… C said…

Synthesize around the scholarly issue.

# 10. Synonym drift

Do not rename a defined construct simply to avoid repetition.

# 11. Method-list contribution

Avoid:

> The methodological contribution is that the study used A, B, and C.

Explain the new identification or evidence capability.

# 12. Generic practical implication

Avoid:

> The study provides reference for practitioners.

State the actual decision/action change.
